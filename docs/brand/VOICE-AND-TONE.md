# Borgi Voice and Tone

## Voice principles

Borgi always sounds clear, warm, concise, and useful. The voice is friendly but not childish, professional but not formal, and playful only when the context is safe.

## Writing rules

- Lead with the outcome or next action.
- Use short sentences and familiar words.
- Prefer active voice.
- Name the object being changed: booking, customer, service, staff member, payment, or reminder.
- State what happened before explaining why.
- Give one primary recovery action for errors.
- Avoid blame, sarcasm, slang, and vague technical language.

## Tone by context

### Success

Warm and confident.

> Booking confirmed. We’ll send a reminder before the appointment.

### Empty state

Helpful and action-oriented.

> No services yet. Add your first service to start accepting bookings.

### Validation

Direct and specific.

> Enter a phone number with 9–10 digits.

### Error

Calm and recoverable.

> We couldn’t save this booking. Check the connection and try again.

### Destructive action

Neutral and explicit.

> Delete this service? Existing appointment records will remain, but customers can no longer book it.

### Payment

Precise and reassuring. Avoid jokes.

> Payment received. A receipt has been sent to the customer.

## Preferred terminology

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Booking | Slot transaction |
| Customer | End user, consumer entity |
| Staff | Resource, operator |
| Service | Product item, SKU |
| Branch | Location node |
| Reminder | Alert blast |
| Cancel booking | Remove schedule |
| Reschedule | Move transaction |

## Mascot dialogue

Borgi may speak in onboarding, empty states, tips, and celebrations. Borgi should not speak in legal notices, payment failures, security alerts, or destructive confirmations.

Good:

> Nothing here yet — Borgi is ready when you are.

Avoid:

> Oopsie! Your payment exploded!
